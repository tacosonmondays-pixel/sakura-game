## 2. Premise

The summer after the second bloom, the sky splits along the Wall at sunrise. On the other side is a school rooftop on
the same hill a thousand years later, and a small girl watering flower boxes. This is **Itomachi**, an ordinary lakeside
city of trains, canals and convenience stores, and Sensei's real home: one foggy spring morning Sensei took its first
ferry with a soggy letter in one pocket, and never arrived. Frozen slimes tumble through the crack, running from a cold
that has swallowed the far north. Outside the ward's lullaby nothing "poofs" home. A creature that falls in Itomachi
stays fallen, and every death in terror tears the sky a little wider.

The Sentinels cross to help, Sensei's old school becomes Shelter 3, and a frightened city answers with guns. A whole
people of freezing refugees comes down the mountain road behind a young oni who carries their last fire. The city's
Gate Response Brigade fires on them, the shrine district burns, and the Brigade crosses into the frozen north to finish
it, where the Sentinels end up fighting their own city's soldiers and learn that first light has nowhere to send a soldier who is already home. Then a
third gate opens in a drowned subway, and survivors in frost-cracked uniforms walk out of **Vesperline**, a
gleaming academy city that will one day stand on this lake. It is already destroyed. Its soul Register lists every
citizen's previous life, so it lists how Itomachi's people die in this war, before they die. Its founding legend is a
nameless student who held a shelter door with a lantern so that four hundred people lived. Its last commander left a
recording for that girl, in Hikari's words, with Hikari's sword in her hand.

Across eight modern acts the cast saves one man the Register has buried and loses a girl only Sensei remembers. They
learn that Hikari, the small girl on the rooftop and the commander on the recording are one soul in three lives. On the
festival night the girl at the door turns out to be the one person nobody thought to protect: **Mahiru**, who believed
she was too small to matter. At the summit, when the Night itself pushes through the Vesper Gate, it is wearing the
frayed scarf the Handicraft Club knitted "for a cat". And when Mahiru's soul passes on, the gates swing. Sensei steps
alone into a living Vesperline years before its fall, where a first-year with Mahiru's habits and none of her memories
hands Sensei a brand-new faculty card, the twin of a cracked one marked MISSING, and says: "...You came."

## 4. The cosmology: one Day, three hours, three gates, one plea

### 4.1 The Long Day

History is not a line. It is one day that goes round. The Morning grows into Noon, Noon grows into the Evening, the
Evening ends in the Night, and after the Night the same Morning comes again: the same lake, the same hill, the same
mountains. Each era knows the one before it as history and the one before that as legend.
- Itomachi tells a folk tale about a city of moon-petals that sank into the lake.
- In Vesperline, *The Quiet Stones* is a picture book about a captain, a mother in the mountains and a little star in a
  hat.
- The Morning knows nothing of either, except what its **founders** hid in "instruction poems". The founders were
  Vesperline's last cadets, who walked through the Vesper Gate on the night of the Fall.

Because the Day goes round, each hour also carries faint **sediment** from earlier turns of the Day. That is why the
Hollow Samurai's shipped lore guards "a shrine that fell long ago" in a Morning where Moonveil still stands, and why
Luna has dreamed all her life of a city burning at noon.

### 4.2 The three gates

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vesper Gate** (future) | Evening → Morning | the Fall: a whole city's deaths at once; held open ever since by Commander Yūhi's dying plea | the founders, a century ago (Morning time); ever since, the Night's cold, and with it the Night's touched (the Rimebound); in Act 8, the Night itself | the 8-4 star rift; the moon leaning north; the hundred-year rhythm |

**Who opened them? The dead, and one plea.** Each gate was torn by one era's dead, and each leads to the next hour of
the Day, which is where those dead were going. What holds all three open, and what pins them to one place in each era,
is a single unanswered request for help. Commander Yūhi died at the Vesper Gate holding a blade of first-light crystal,
asking for someone. Her plea pinned the Merge to her own soul, so the gates open onto the three hard seasons of that
soul's three lives: Hikari's cold spring, Mahiru's war autumn, Yūhi's Night. That is why the first crack opens between
the Wall and a rooftop at the minute Hikari and Mahiru both watch the sun come up. The player sees this in Act 1 and
is told in Act 7.

**Why now?**
1. The Night has always pressed on the Vesper Gate. The Morning's ward held it shut at the hundred-year swing.
2. This spring the ward was dim, because the Hearthstone was keeping the eggs warm (shipped, unchanged). The Night's
   cold seeped through the star rift: the spring was cold, the mother woke early, and the moon leaned north toward the
   rift. Arc 0 answered where the warmth went; this answers why the cold came.
3. In `ch8.epi` the Hearthstone blazed and shut the Vesper Gate out of the Morning again. **Relighting was right**, and
   the ward was also keeping the hours apart. But the cold already inside pooled in the far north, outside the ward's
   ring, and the Emberfolk began to freeze. Their deaths tore the Petal Gate.
4. *Why the Night pressed harder this time* is Arc 2's question (§17).

### 4.3 The rules (strict; the story never breaks them)

1. **One Day, one circle.** No parallel worlds, no second copies of an era. The one Day can be rewritten, and then the
   rewrite is the only version.
2. **The Day rhymes.** Each hour lives the same story in its own way: a cold season, a people at a warm door, a keeper
   who holds a light, someone small who stays. This is why "the portals retell the same story", why every modern act
   retells its fantasy chapter with another ending, and why Luna's "It always does" is true.
3. **One soul, one thread, one life in each hour.** When a life ends, its thread passes forward into the next hour, into
   someone new. That is "reborn in the next loop". No memories pass. Echoes do: a gesture, a fear, a turn of phrase, a
   taste in tea.
4. **Death is real.** Nobody comes back, by a gate, a rewrite or a soul-thread. Kinu's grandmother's saying: "The
   thread goes on. The stitch is finished."
5. **The ward is a lullaby that keeps threads uncut.** Inside a lit wardstone's light, a falling creature is not cut but
   sent home: the shipped "poof". Outside, falling is final. Itomachi's wardstones still exist, hidden in things people
   kept loving for a thousand years; they are dark, and they can be relit. **The Sentinels fight inside first light:**
   wherever the Dawnblade is on the field, a creature the girls knock down goes home through the nearest seam. The
   Brigade's guns carry no such light. **First light was sung for the wilds:** it sends home what a seam brought out of
   the wilds (every Petal Gate family, Kagari's people among them), and it has nowhere to send a soldier of Itomachi,
   who is already home. A soldier who falls, inside first light or out, stays fallen (shown, not named, in Act 4).
6. **A thread cut in terror snags, and a snag is a gate.** One death makes a pinhole that crackles and heals. Many make a
   door that stays. **Killing makes the Merge worse.** HAMMER tears the sky every time it fires.
7. **The circle must close.** You *can* change what happens. But a change that contradicts another hour **unweaves**
   whatever depended on it, people included. An unwoven person has not died: she has never been. Just before it happens
   she **flickers**. Only the Constant remembers her afterwards. **The Night is the knot in the circle:** a change
   ripples forward one hour at a time, so a change at Noon reaches the Evening at once, but it can reach the next Morning
   only by undoing the Night. That is why in Arc 1 only the Evening's people flicker (Toki, Tsubaki, Tenka), even though
   Hikari is Yūhi's next life, and why in Arc 2, where the Night itself is at stake, Hikari begins to flicker.
8. **Two lives of one soul can share an hour.** A traveller keeps her own thread, so through a gate a girl can stand
   beside her own past or next life. They do not know each other, but echoes bleed between them (dreams, a feeling, a
   habit).
9. **The gates.** While the Merge holds, the three hours run side by side: a day in Itomachi is a day in Moonpetal and a
   day in Vesperline's ruins. Anyone can cross, but a gate throws a traveller toward warmth (which is why cold creatures
   pour into a heated city); **only the Constant arrives where they mean to**, and anyone holding Sensei's hand arrives
   with them. The gates follow the soul the Merge is pinned to. When that pinned life ends, the pin slips forward to the
   thread's next life, and the gates swing to the season where that life's trouble begins.
10. **The Constant.** Sensei has no thread, exists once in every hour, is never reborn, walks any gate in either
    direction, has a clock that only runs forward, and **remembers whatever is unwoven**. Where no gate stands, only two
    things have ever crossed: a plea for help asked of the water in a hard season of the pinned soul, which surfaces
    wherever the Constant is, and the Constant, following it. That is how Sensei first came, in spring, before any gate
    opened at Noon: Hikari's letter surfaced in Itomachi's plaza fountain, and Sensei followed it on the dawn ferry into
    the fog. Nothing and nobody else can cross that way. Nobody knows why (Arc 2 and later).

**Teaching plan:** no rule is explained before the player has seen it happen, and at most one rule is named per act.
Rule 5 is shown in Act 1, and its edge (first light has nowhere to send someone already home) is felt on 4-8 and seen in `g4.cross`; rule 6
is shown in Acts 1 and 3 and named in Act 4; rules 3, 4 and 7 come in Act 5; rule 8 in
Act 6; rule 2 is named by Yoriko's play in Act 7; rule 9's pin is the finale (rule 10's one gateless crossing is shown,
not named, in the Act 8 side scene "Ask the Water"). Tenka explains each rule on a whiteboard
from Act 5 on, and each becomes a Captain's Journal page with Hikari's doodles (kind `lesson`, 0 gems).

## 6. Sensei: the Constant

**Role.** Sensei taught at Hilltop for one week of spring, until the foggy morning of the first ferry. In Itomachi,
Sensei is a missing person. A sun-faded poster hangs at the station, and the photo is always covered by something: a
pigeon, a slime, a parking sticker, and at the very end a Doorie sticker. The rule that Sensei's face is never shown
becomes a joke the whole city is in on. In the war Sensei is the only person who walks every gate cleanly, reads every
field and remembers every unwoven life. As in Arc 0, Sensei speaks only through one choice per episode at most, always
kind. Narration may say what Sensei remembers or notices, never what Sensei looks like. Gender, age and face are never
shown or stated (owner question Q1).

**Sensei's arc** (written so the player can project onto it):
- **Wants** to protect both homes: the Morning that took Sensei in, and the Noon that Sensei left without a word.
- **Fears** that answering the letter emptied Sensei's first home of its teacher. The old class is furious; Ms. Hazuki
  covered every lesson.
- **Gets wrong, part 1:** Sensei sees Hikari in Mahiru ("a small Captain") and never quite sees *Mahiru*. The player's
  own choices make the comparison, warmly. Neither Sensei nor the player imagines that she could be the girl at the door.
- **Gets wrong, part 2:** Sensei keeps secrets to spare people. First the little one in the boiler room (Act 2), then
  Toki, whom only Sensei remembers (Act 5). Sensei starts to carry alone, which is the Commander's flaw in a teacher's
  key. The first secret has a price: had Hikari known, the little one could have gone home in Act 2.
- **Turns:** Act 5's epilogue. Both choices end with Sensei telling everything, because the squad must know what a
  rescue costs before Act 7. Hikari answers with her own lesson grown one size larger: "Stand with me means *tell* me."
- **Changes:** after Mahiru, Sensei becomes what only the Constant can be, the one who remembers each person as
  *herself*. At the memorial both options of the choice say the arc's thesis: **"She was Mahiru."** Not "Yūhi's past
  life", not "Hikari's next life". Each life is its own person.
- **Ends:** stepping alone into a living Vesperline, knowing exactly how the first-year in front of them will die, and
  choosing to be her teacher anyway. The Commander's last recording asked Sensei to "come earlier next time". Sensei
  does. The card in Sensei's hand says Sensei will still be there at the end.

**Relationships that shift:** the old class (furious → "You came back"); Hikari (partners → hurt by what Sensei kept →
equals who tell each other everything); Mahiru (an echo of Hikari → a person, too late); Tenka (the one who explains →
the one who was paid for, like Sensei); Kagari (an enemy → the person Hikari asked); Yūhi (a voice on a recording → a

### 8.1 Hikari (Morning · Dawn thread · adult hero)
- **Wants** to keep "Stand with me" true in a war.
- **Fears** being the reason someone falls.
- **Gets wrong:** war pulls her straight back to "Stay behind me". When the board points at Azami's door, her instinct
  for the small first-year is protection: she sends Mahiru to the back of the shelter, out of the fighting, and that
  puts Mahiru beside the back door. After Mahiru she tries to end the war alone (Act 8), which is the Commander's
  mistake made by her next life.
- **Carries:** the hand she takes off the hilt in Act 1 is a choice, not a rule, and she keeps making it where she can.
  First light sends the wilds home but has nowhere to send a soldier of Itomachi, so the soldiers who fall on 4-8 stay fallen. Her answer is
  "Hold back wherever you can. Anyone who runs, let them run." (`g4.cross`). On 4-9, with the squad's guns on the
  village's last warm hearths, she draws anyway, and again on 7-8 and 7-9. She takes every one of those falls as hers
  alone ("Then it's mine. Not yours."), which is the Commander's flaw in her own hand. It is why she sends Mahiru
  "behind me" on the night of the parade, and why setting the sword down in Act 8 costs her something.
- **Turns:** Luna's apology and the squad walking up the mountain road behind her ("Not this time, Sunshine.").
- **Changes:** she sets the Dawnblade down in front of Kagari, and in front of Kuroe, whose soldiers it took, and asks her
  enemy for help, because the enemy is the only other person trying to keep children warm. Her lesson moves from Arc
  0's "nobody has to protect everyone alone" to Arc 1's **"standing together doesn't mean nobody falls. It means
  nobody falls alone."**
- **Relationships:** a big sister to Mahiru, then her grief ("She's who I'll be."); "I won't be you," to the recording;
  Sensei, hurt by secrets, then equals; Luna, from "You knew" to forgiveness; Kagari, from enemy to the person she
  asked. Her core never breaks: she never gives up, never blames a student, never wins alone.

### 8.2 Mahiru (Noon · Dawn thread · story character, not a unit by default, Q2)
A first-year at Hilltop who had Sensei as homeroom teacher for one week. She tends the rooftop flower boxes because
nobody else does, apologises to furniture she bumps into, carries things for everyone, and taps twice over her heart
before she says anything brave.
- **Wants** to be useful; secretly, to *matter*.
- **Fears** that she is too small to make a difference, and in the way if she tries.
- **Gets wrong:** she makes herself small. She never asks for help, never steps forward, and keeps the little one in the
  boiler room secret because asking feels like being a bother. That secret is how it is lost.
- **Turns:** Ms. Hazuki goes back into the burning Old Town for her (Act 3), and Mahiru cannot understand why anyone
  would. On the rooftop at dawn Hikari and Sensei tell her she matters now (Act 7).
- **Changes:** at the shelter's back door the motor has frozen, the street is torn open and pouring cold, and people are
  still coming. She could go inside. She stays outside with her festival lantern so they can find the way, until the
  last one is in and the door can close. Her last words, over the intercom: "Captain? I'm not scared. ...Okay, I'm a
  little scared. Is that allowed?" / "It's allowed."
- **Relationships:** Sensei (the teacher who came back); Hikari (her idol, and her past life); the voice on the radio
  (her idol, and her next life); Rei (the rooftop flowers; Rei keeps them after); Tenka (Doorie); Kinu (the scarf "for
  a cat"); Azami (who shoos her off the roof every day and lets her stay every day).

### 8.3 Commander Yūhi (Evening · Dawn thread · dead before Arc 1; heard in recordings)
The last commander of Vesperline's Student Defence Corps. The player assembles her arc from broadcasts that have looped
through the gates since the Fall:
- **Early broadcasts** (Act 2), bright and sure: "Vesperline holds. Get some sleep, everyone. I've got it."
- **Middle logs** (Act 4 side, Act 5 slate), tired: she sends everyone else to rest and takes every watch herself.
- **The last recording** (Act 6), at the Vesper Gate, holding it open for the last cadets (the future founders) with
  the Dawnblade in her hand.

*Her flaw* was carrying everyone. *Her change* came at the end, too late, and that is the point. She found her own
prior-life entry in the Register, read how the girl at the door died, and redacted it so the girl would never read her
own ending. The recording: *"They made you into a mascot. You'd have hated that. You were small, and you held it
anyway. I couldn't protect everyone alone. I should have asked. You never had to be big. You only had to stay."* Then:
*"Sensei, if this reaches you: come earlier next time."* Her dying plea is what pins the Merge (§4.2).

### 8.5 Kagari, the Ember Keeper (Morning, far north · Ember thread · the recurring rival)
A young oni (no age stated) who became keeper of the Ember when her mother froze holding it. Formal, proud, dutiful and
very tired. She never makes war on children: in Act 3 she sends captured Hilltop students home with a message.
- **Wants** her people warm by spring.
- **Fears** the fire going out on her watch.
- **Gets wrong:** she takes warmth by force, because asking never worked for her mother, and she holds on to

- **Seen across the arc:** a figure on the far shore (Act 3); the parley over Azami's barrier (Act 4); her own camp, at

- **Changes:** she lets go. She gives the Ember to the summit stone so the city's ward can wake, and leads her people

  own thread.
- **Voice:** a named NPC, not bound by the six-word monster rule. Full formal sentences, never a contraction: "I do not
  ask. My mother asked the winter. The winter did not answer."

### 8.6 Azami (Noon · Ember thread · free unit · the red herring)
Hilltop's checkpoint prefect: strict about rules and secretly soft on first-years ("No running in the halls — or on my

## 9. The mystery

### 9.1 The questions

The central mystery is a set of nested questions, each asked only after the last has a shape:
1. **Who is the Commander talking to?** (Acts 2-6.) A voice from a dead city uses Hikari's words.
2. **Who is the girl at the door?** (Acts 5-7.) The legend, the mascot, the Register's redacted line.
3. **Who is Mahiru?** The question the player never thinks to ask, and the answer to the first two.

Under them run two more: **where does the cold come from?** (the shipped question: Vesperline's Night in Act 6, and in
Act 8 the Night is the little one), and **why is Sensei the Constant?** (Arc 2 and later; Arc 1 only sharpens it).

### 9.3 The mid-arc reveal (Act 5): "It's already written."

In the drowned subway the Noon Gate's mouth breathes frost and the Last Watch walks out. Kohaku reads Tenka's cracked
slate, and three things land in one episode:
1. **Vesperline is Itomachi's future.** It stands on this lake and was built by the children of this war's survivors.
2. **Vesperline is gone.** The cast is fighting for the past of a city that has already died.
3. **This war's dead are already in the Register,** listed as Vesperline citizens' previous lives.

Hikari: "Then we unwrite it." The Sentinels pull Lieutenant Mori out of the reservoir. Toki, whistling four notes
beside Tenka, is gone between one line and the next, and only Sensei remembers her. The rest of the arc lives with what
that means: saving someone the record has buried is possible, and someone else always pays.

*What it re-reads:* the static roll call (Act 2); the Commander's broadcasts (a dead woman, not a distant one); every
monster so far (people running from the same cold that ended the future); HAMMER (each barrage tore a gate, so the war has
been feeding itself); the Brigade's crate (Vesperline's records are about to say where it went).

### 9.4 The late twist (Act 7): "The name is Mahiru."

On the festival night the squad guards Shelter 3's main door with Azami, because the board says she is the Door Girl.
The battle is won, and in that breath Kohaku finishes decoding the Commander's redacted entry: *Prior life: MAHIRU. Place: Hilltop
Shelter 3, east service door.* That is the back door, where Hikari sent the small first-year to be safe.

*What it re-reads:*
- The voice Mahiru adored was her own next life, speaking to her.
- The legend the Last Watch grew up on was about her; the keychain on her bag is the mascot of her own death.
- Hikari's protective order put her beside the door.
- Kage's board missed her because Mahiru herself believed she did not count, and so did everyone, the player included.
- With P5 the circle closes. Hikari is the Commander's next life and Mahiru is Hikari's: Hikari is running toward the
  girl she will one day become.

Hikari runs. Tenka and Tsubaki flicker, because if Mahiru lives the Evening that made them comes apart. Tenka: "Go
anyway." The door is closing when Hikari gets there. "Is that allowed?" "It's allowed." Then quiet. Tenka and Tsubaki
steady, and both of them have to live with why.

### 9.5 The finale reveal (Act 8): "That's my stitch."

At the summit the Night pours through the Vesper Gate in a shape. Grey wool trails in its shadow, and Kinu knows her
own stitch. The Night is the little one from the boiler room, kept warm for centuries as the heart of something that
would not let go, until it grew cold and enormous. Its cold made Arc 0's spring cold, woke its own mother early and
hatched its own egg (P6).

*What it re-reads:* the boiler-room scenes of Act 2; Sensei's first secret; the crate in Act 5; the nest and the wool in
Act 6; the shipped bestiary line "It carries her inside its shadow"; Hikari's promise "Then we'll keep them warm too";
and the warm white tower that will glow over Vesperline in the cliffhanger.

## 16. The finale and the cliffhanger

**8-10, and "First Light, Twice" (`g8.after`).** The three gates line up over the summit, and the Night pours through
the Vesper Gate. Hikari has already set down her sword in front of her enemy and asked; Azami has lifted her barrier;
Kagari's people, the Brigade and the Sentinels hold the mountain road together (the Brigade's searchlights on the summit,
its foam on the wyverns' fire, Herons circling the gates). At the battle's last phase the shipped banner plays,
"The eclipse breaks — the Ashwing Matriarch tears free!": the mother who has searched the north since summer breaks her
child's shadow with her warmth, and the Night flinches back through the gate. The Ember and the Dawnblade wake the
summit stone, and Itomachi's ward hums for the first time in a thousand years. The Emberfolk walk home through the Petal
Gate into a warm Morning, and the Matriarch carries the smallest over the pass. Kuroe, who held fire at the
checkpoint, reads a roll in which every name answers, stands HAMMER down for good, answers Nami's letter, and tells the
truth about where the little one went. Hikari: "Then I'll find it."

**"She Was Mahiru" (`g8.epi`).**
1. **Hilltop, the memorial.** Kinu's banner holds a stitched row for every name. Sensei asks for one more, and Kinu sews
   "Toki", a name she doesn't remember. Rei's ledger page. Yuzuha falls asleep on Sensei's desk ("...Deploying.
   ...zzz"), the first sign of peace.
2. Sensei's choice, where both options say it: "She was Mahiru." / "Her name was Mahiru." Hikari hangs the Doorie
   keychain on the rooftop flower box.
3. **The Wall at sunrise**, a bookend to Arc 0. "Sensei! Up here — on the wall! ...Bring the whole squad! It's a very
   long wall." They all come: the Sentinels, the Hilltop Watch, the Last Watch. Kagari watches from the gate. Hikari taps
   twice over her heart. There is one space on the Wall that nobody fills, and nobody mentions it.

**The cliffhanger: "Come Earlier" (`g8.end`).**
1. **The pin slips.** Mahiru's thread has passed on. Above Hilltop's rooftop the Noon Gate shudders, and the frozen
   ruins on the far side melt into **morning**: a bright, living Vesperline, sky-rails running, cherry trees in glass
   courtyards, students with halos hurrying to class. Tenka, very quietly: "That's before. That's years before."
2. Hikari, to Sensei, and only to Sensei: "Go. Someone asked."
3. **Sensei goes through alone,** as only the Constant can.
4. A first-year with a Vesperline badge runs round a corner, collides with Sensei, apologises three times, and then
   stops and taps twice over her heart. Her badge reads **YŪHI**. She remembers nothing. She is someone new. "Are you
   the new teacher? ...That's weird. I feel like I've been waiting for you."
5. She hands over a card from the welcome desk: **SENSEI · Faculty Advisor · Student Defence Corps.** Brand new. In
   Sensei's other pocket is its cracked twin: *MISSING since the Fall.*
6. Above the city the white tower glows a warm amber, and the public address system says, gently: *"Good morning,
   Vesperline. Thanks to the Spindle, no one you love will ever have to leave."*
7. Yūhi looks up at Sensei. **"...You came."**

Cut to black. "End of Arc 1 · The Girl at the Door."

**Why it lands:** it is the Commander's plea finally answered, and it is the cruelest kindness the rules allow. Sensei
has come earlier, as asked, and knows by heart how this girl will die. The card says Sensei will still be in this city
when it falls. The PA line means something very different to a player who knows that a soul that never passes on cannot
be reborn, and the warm glow in the tower is the exact colour of a boiler room. **Why it does not cheapen Arc 1:**
nobody comes back. Yūhi is not Mahiru restored; she is someone new, and "She was Mahiru" was said before the gate
opened. **Why it works for a player-avatar game:** the paradox is now the player's own. Sensei is on record in a future
Sensei has not lived yet.
